## 1. Reconciliation sequence

Work from original records where available:

1. trial balance and general ledger to financial statements;
2. tax filings and invoices to reported revenue/cost;
3. bank statements to cash, debt, funding, and major payments;
4. contract ledger to delivery, acceptance, revenue, invoice, and cash;
5. customer/supplier ledgers to confirmations and interviews;
6. payroll/headcount to R&D, sales, and management costs;
7. capex, inventory, and fixed assets to physical existence and utilization.

Document every unreconciled difference and quantify its effect.

## 2. Core analyses

- Three to five years of income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow when available.
- Revenue growth by product, customer, geography, channel, and recurring/non-recurring type.
- Gross margin bridge: price, volume, mix, input cost, labor, subcontracting, warranty, and capitalization.
- Expense quality: headcount, one-off items, related parties, capitalization, grants, and classification changes.
- Working capital: DSO, aging, overdue concentration, inventory turns/obsolescence, prepayments, payables, and contract liabilities.
- Cash flow: EBITDA-to-OCF bridge, cash burn, runway, debt service, restricted cash, and committed funding.
- Off-balance-sheet exposure, guarantees, litigation, tax, leases, repurchase obligations, and contingent consideration.
- Related-party transactions and circular or pass-through fund flows.

## 3. Revenue quality

Build a customer-level bridge:

`contract value -> deliverable -> delivery date -> acceptance evidence -> accounting policy -> recognized revenue -> invoice -> cash -> outstanding balance`

Flag:

- large contract values with limited invoices or cash;
- acceptance controlled by the seller or unsupported by customer evidence;
- channel stuffing, bill-and-hold, side letters, returns, discounts, or financing arrangements;
- customers that are also suppliers, shareholders, employees, or financing conduits;
- R&D/sample orders presented as recurring commercial demand;
- long implementation or validation periods that defer revenue and cash.

## 4. Forecast and funding

Build from operational drivers. At minimum show:

- price, volume, conversion, delivery, acceptance, and collection assumptions;
- capacity, yield, hiring, sales cycle, regulatory, and technical milestones;
- gross margin by product or service;
- headcount and R&D/capex plans;
- monthly or quarterly cash requirement until the next value-inflection milestone;
- downside/base/upside cases and break-even sensitivity.

Use probability weighting for pipeline and regulatory/technical milestones. Do not treat the management budget as the analyst base case without independent support.

## 5. Red flags

- unpaid registered capital or inconsistent cap table/funding records;
- loans routed through suppliers or unusual reciprocal transfers;
- customer/supplier overlap without commercial explanation;
- revenue, tax, invoice, and cash mismatch;
- rapid receivable/inventory growth versus revenue;
- declining gross margin hidden by forecast claims;
- related-party dependence or undisclosed guarantees;
- negative operating cash flow funded by short-term debt;
- audit adjustments, late filings, weak segregation of duties, or missing original records.

Translate material findings into valuation adjustments, staged funding, conditions precedent, representations, covenants, indemnities, or a decline recommendation.
