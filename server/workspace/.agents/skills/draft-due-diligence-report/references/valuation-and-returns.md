## 1. Method selection

Choose methods appropriate to stage and business model:

- revenue/ARR multiples for recurring or high-growth businesses with credible revenue quality;
- EBITDA/EBIT multiples for established operations;
- DCF only when long-term cash flow assumptions can be defended;
- transaction comparables when terms, stage, date, and market conditions are comparable;
- milestone or probability-adjusted valuation for pre-revenue technology, biotech, medical-device, regulatory, or long-cycle projects;
- asset/replacement-cost or sum-of-the-parts when operating metrics are not meaningful.

Do not use a method merely because it produces the desired answer.

## 2. Comparable-company discipline

For each comparable record:

- exact entity and security;
- valuation date and source;
- currency and FX date;
- enterprise/equity value distinction;
- revenue/earnings period and actual/forecast status;
- business mix, geography, customer type, growth, margin, stage, liquidity, and capital intensity;
- adjustments and reason for inclusion.

Separate public-company, private-round, and M&A comparables. Exclude or cap outliers; show median and interquartile range. Never apply a mega-cap or platform multiple to an early-stage project without a transparent discount and milestone bridge.

## 3. Scenario model

Use at least downside, base, and upside cases. Link each case to operational milestones, not arbitrary percentages. Show:

- revenue/profit/cash at exit;
- exit year and multiple;
- technical/regulatory/commercial success probability when relevant;
- future financing and dilution;
- preference and liquidation effects;
- resulting equity value and investor proceeds.

Stress exit delay, multiple compression, lower conversion, lower margin, higher cash need, and an additional financing round.

## 4. Return model

Calculate:

- initial and follow-on investment;
- fully diluted ownership after each round;
- investor proceeds by scenario;
- MOIC;
- gross IRR using dated cash flows;
- probability-weighted expected value when probabilities are supportable;
- downside loss and recovery path.

Do not treat contractual repurchase as risk-free. Assess obligor capacity, liability cap, seniority, enforceability, and competing claims.

## 5. Presentation

Present one compact scenario table followed by the key assumptions and the variables to which the decision is most sensitive. State whether the entry valuation leaves an adequate margin of safety relative to verified current performance and required future milestones.
