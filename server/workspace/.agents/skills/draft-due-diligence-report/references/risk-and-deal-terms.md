# Risk and Deal Terms

## Contents

1. Risk statement
2. Risk matrix
3. Control hierarchy
4. Transaction mapping
5. Residual risk

## 1. Risk statement

Write risks as falsifiable events with evidence and impact. Avoid labels such as “市场风险” without mechanism.

Good pattern:

`Current fact -> failure event/trigger -> probability -> financial/legal/strategic impact -> evidence -> control -> remaining exposure`

## 2. Risk matrix

Use these fields:

- risk ID and category;
- current fact and evidence IDs;
- trigger/threshold;
- probability and rationale;
- impact and quantified exposure;
- pre-closing action;
- post-closing monitoring indicator;
- owner and deadline;
- required evidence;
- contractual remedy;
- residual risk and effect on recommendation.

Prioritize thesis-breaking and value-impairing risks. Do not inflate the list with generic boilerplate.

## 3. Control hierarchy

Prefer controls in this order:

1. avoid/decline the exposure;
2. resolve before signing or closing;
3. change price or structure;
4. stage funding against objective milestones;
5. obtain representation, warranty, indemnity, escrow, holdback, or specific remedy;
6. impose covenant, information right, board/reserved-matter protection, or use-of-funds control;
7. monitor after closing;
8. provide optional operating assistance.

“投后赋能” is not a substitute for contractual risk control.

## 4. Transaction mapping

Map findings to:

- conditions precedent;
- conditions to each funding tranche;
- valuation adjustment or convertible structure;
- use-of-funds and account supervision;
- founder full-time/IP assignment/non-compete obligations;
- customer/order/acceptance/cash milestones;
- regulatory/technical milestones;
- cap table cleanup and related-party separation;
- representations and warranties;
- specific indemnity;
- liquidation preference, anti-dilution, pre-emption, information, board, veto, tag/drag, and exit rights;
- walk-away triggers.

State responsible party, deadline, evidence of completion, and consequence of failure.

## 5. Residual risk

After controls, reassess probability and impact. Do not call risk “controllable” merely because a mitigation is listed. Explain what cannot be transferred or monitored and how that changes position size, valuation, structure, reserve, or recommendation.
